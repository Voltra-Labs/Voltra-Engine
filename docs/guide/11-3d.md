# 11. 3D

0.5 adds a 3D world beside the 2D one. It is being built stage by stage,
and this chapter grows with it: where things are, how they hang off each
other, a camera that draws meshes (stage 135) and — since stage 136 — 2D
and 3D cameras drawing one frame together.

There is no 3D view in the editor yet. A scene can hold 3D entities, save
them, load them, parent them, spawn them from prefabs and, through a
`Camera3D`, draw them in the player, a headless screenshot and the
editor's Game view, which shows the whole frame — both worlds and the
HUD; the editor's Scene view stays 2D and says in its toolbar how many 3D
entities it is not showing.

## Two worlds in one scene

2D and 3D are two parallel subsystems in one engine, the way Godot keeps
`Node2D` and `Node3D`: a 2D entity carries a `Transform2D`, a 3D one a
`Transform3D`, and everything else — names, the hierarchy, prefabs,
scenes, audio, input, the UI — is shared. Nothing locks a project to one:
a scene holds whichever entities it holds.

2D is never 3D flattened. A `Transform2D` has no z, the 2D renderer has no
depth buffer, and draw order in 2D is still `sort_order`.

## Coordinates

- **Y is up, the world is right-handed, forward is −Z, and a unit is a
  metre.** That is glTF's convention (and Godot's and Bevy's), so a model
  exported from Blender as glTF arrives the right way up and the right
  size with no conversion.
- Up means the same in both worlds: 2D is Y up too.
- A camera looks down its entity's −Z, as a light will.

## `Transform3D`

```ron
"Transform3D": (
    translation: (0.0, 1.5, -4.0),
    rotation: (0.0, 0.70710677, 0.0, 0.70710677),
    scale: (1.0, 1.0, 1.0),
),
```

- **`translation`**: where the entity is, in metres, relative to its parent.
- **`rotation`**: a quaternion `(x, y, z, w)` — exact, no gimbal lock. A
  file is text you can edit: a quaternion typed by hand that is not quite
  unit length is normalised as it loads, and one that is not a rotation at
  all (all zeros) loads as no rotation. The Console says so once.
- **`scale`**: per axis; `(1, 1, 1)` leaves the size.

Any field left out takes its default, so `"Transform3D": (translation: (0.0,
1.0, 0.0))` is a whole transform.

In the **inspector**, the rotation is three angles in degrees — about X, Y
and Z, applied Y first, then X, then Z (yaw, pitch, roll; Godot's order).
What you type stays what you see: a yaw of 180 stays 180, though the
quaternion underneath could be read back as a pitch and a roll of 180 each.

In code:

```rust
use voltra_scene::Transform3D;
use voltra_render::glam::Vec3;

let lamp = Transform3D::from_translation(Vec3::new(0.0, 2.0, 0.0));
let turned = Transform3D::from_euler_degrees(Vec3::new(0.0, 90.0, 0.0));
let aimed = lamp.looking_at(Vec3::ZERO, Vec3::Y);
assert!(aimed.forward().y < 0.0); // looking down at the origin
```

`forward()`, `right()` and `up()` are the entity's −Z, +X and +Y in its
parent's space; `matrix()` is the `Affine3A` it composes as (scale, then
rotate, then translate).

## Making a 3D entity

**Hierarchy ▸ Create ▸ 3D ▸ Empty** makes one at the origin, named
`Empty3D`. Give it components in the inspector, or through the editor's
bridge and the MCP server, which set `Transform3D` like any other component.

## Parenting

A `Transform3D` is local to its parent, as a `Transform2D` is: move the
parent and the children come with it, turned by its rotation and grown by
its scale. `hierarchy::world_matrix_3d` gives one entity's composed matrix;
`WorldTransforms3D::from_world` composes the whole world in one pass.

The rules:

- **A chain keeps to one dimension.** A 3D entity cannot be the child of a
  2D one, or the parent of one; the hierarchy panel shows such a drop in
  red and refuses it. There is nothing to compose: a 2D offset means
  nothing in 3D.
- **An entity with no transform passes the rule through.** A bare entity
  can group either kind — but once it holds 3D children it cannot go under
  a 2D parent, and the reverse.
- **An entity is 2D or 3D, not both.** The inspector and the bridge will
  not add a `Transform3D` to an entity that has a `Transform2D`.
- A file that breaks a rule anyway (a hand edit, a merge) opens with a
  warning in the Console and is left as it is; each world composes the
  entity by its own transform, as if the other one's link were not there.
- Physics and parenting stay exclusive in 2D, and there is no 3D physics
  yet.

## Prefabs

A 3D prefab works as a 2D one does. Spawn it where it should stand by
passing a `Transform3D`:

```rust
let crate_prefab = tick.prefab(&AssetPath::new("prefabs/crate.prefab.ron")?);
let at = Transform3D::from_translation(Vec3::new(4.0, 0.0, -2.0));
tick.spawn(crate_prefab, at)?;
```

or `Placement::AsAuthored` to leave its roots where the file put them. A
2D placement does not move a 3D root (it stays where its file put it, with
a warning), so dropping a 3D prefab on the 2D scene view spawns it as
authored.

As in 2D, an instance root's transform is its placement and never an
override: move it and nothing is recorded against the prefab, **Apply**
keeps the file's own root transform, and **Create prefab** from a 3D
selection stores the root at the origin with its rotation and scale kept.

## Animation and networking

A clip keys a 3D transform with `Translation3D`, `Rotation3D` and
`Scale3D`. A rotation key is a quaternion, `Quat((x, y, z, w))` in the
file; between two keys it turns the short way round, so key a spin every
quarter turn or so. Crossfades, blend trees and layers mix rotations by
`slerp`, and an additive layer adds its turn on top of what is under it:

```ron
(looping: true, length: 2.0, tracks: [
    (property: Rotation3D, keys: [
        (time: 0.0, value: Quat((0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0))),
        (time: 1.0, value: Quat((0.0, 0.7071068, 0.0, 0.7071068))),
        (time: 2.0, value: Quat((0.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0))),
    ]),
])
```

The Timeline lists the three properties and edits a rotation key as Euler
degrees; authoring 3D clips properly comes with the 3D view.

Over the network a `Transform3D` is replicated as a `Transform2D` is:
remote copies are drawn between snapshots, your own predicted player is
corrected to the server, and interest management culls by distance in
space (chapter 8).

## A first 3D scene

Four entities are a 3D scene you can look at: a camera, a sun, a floor
and a cube. Written by hand into `assets/scenes/first3d.ron`:

```ron
(
    version: 2,
    entities: [
        (
            id: "019fe300-0135-7000-8000-0000000000a1",
            components: {
                "Name": "Camera",
                "Camera3D": (),
                "Transform3D": (
                    translation: (0.0, 2.0, 5.0),
                    rotation: (-0.1891, 0.0, 0.0, 0.982),
                ),
            },
        ),
        (
            id: "019fe300-0135-7000-8000-0000000000a4",
            components: {
                "Name": "Sun",
                "DirectionalLight3D": (),
                "Transform3D": (rotation: (-0.4031, 0.2725, 0.1271, 0.8644)),
            },
        ),
        (
            id: "019fe300-0135-7000-8000-0000000000a2",
            components: {
                "Name": "Floor",
                "MeshInstance3D": (mesh: "builtin:plane", color: (0.35, 0.37, 0.4, 1.0)),
                "Transform3D": (scale: (10.0, 1.0, 10.0)),
            },
        ),
        (
            id: "019fe300-0135-7000-8000-0000000000a3",
            components: {
                "Name": "Cube",
                "MeshInstance3D": (mesh: "builtin:cube", color: (0.9, 0.15, 0.1, 1.0)),
                "Transform3D": (translation: (0.0, 0.5, 0.0)),
            },
        ),
    ],
)
```

and run with `cargo run -p voltra-player -- assets/scenes/first3d.ron`.
The camera stands 5 m back and 2 m up, pitched down 21.8° (the quaternion
about X) to look at the origin, where the cube stands; the sun shines down
its entity's −Z, turned 50° down and 35° round so it lights the faces the
camera sees; the floor is the 1 m built-in plane scaled to 10 m; the cube
sits on it, its centre half a metre up. Both meshes are drawn through the
default material, a white dielectric tinted by their `color` (below).
`scenes/shapes3d.ron` is a bigger version with every built-in mesh,
overlapping, drawn unlit in flat colours.

The same in the editor: **Hierarchy ▸ Create ▸ 3D ▸ Camera** (5 m back and
2 m up, looking at the origin), **▸ Directional Light**, **▸ Plane** and
**▸ Cube** (each standing on the ground at the origin); scale the plane in
the inspector, then switch the viewport to **Game** to see it through the
camera.

### `Camera3D`

```ron
"Camera3D": (
    projection: Perspective(fov_y_degrees: 60.0, near: 0.1, far: None),
    priority: 0,
    active: true,
    clear: Background,
    viewport: (x: 0.0, y: 0.0, width: 1.0, height: 1.0),
    tonemap: AgX,
    post: (exposure: 1.0, contrast: 1.0, saturation: 1.0),
),
```

- **`projection`**: `Perspective` — `fov_y_degrees`, the view's height as an
  angle (60 by default); `near`, the nearest distance drawn (0.1 m);
  `far`, the furthest, or `None` (the default) for no far plane at all —
  or `Orthographic(size: 5.0, near: 0.1, far: 1000.0)`, `size` being half
  the height it shows, in metres, as a `Camera2D`'s is.
- **`priority`** and **`active`**: every active camera draws, lowest
  priority first, a later one over an earlier (below).
- **`clear`**: `Background` (the default) or `Color((r, g, b, a))`, what
  the picture starts as. Over a 2D camera that drew first the background
  is transparent; a colour of its own keeps it opaque.
- **`viewport`**: the part of the screen it draws, as a `Camera2D`'s: all
  of it by default; `(x: 0.5, y: 0.0, width: 0.5, height: 1.0)` is the
  right half.
- **`tonemap`**: how light is brought into the screen's range — `AgX`
  (the default), `Aces`, `Reinhard` or `None` (below).
- **`post`**: exposure, contrast, saturation, tint, vignette and bloom, as
  a `Camera2D`'s.

Every field has its default, so `"Camera3D": ()` is a whole camera. It
looks down its entity's −Z and ignores the entity's scale. In code,
`Transform3D::looking_at` points it:

```rust
use voltra_render::glam::Vec3;
use voltra_scene::{Camera3D, Transform3D};

let eye = Transform3D::from_translation(Vec3::new(0.0, 2.0, 5.0))
    .looking_at(Vec3::ZERO, Vec3::Y);
let camera = world.spawn();
world.insert(camera, eye);
world.insert(camera, Camera3D::default());
```

A camera inside a mesh sees nothing of it: a mesh's back faces are not
drawn, unless its material is `double_sided` (below). A projection that cannot be drawn as written — a near plane of 0,
a far plane in front of the near one — is drawn with that field
corrected, and the inspector and the Console say which.

**Depth.** What is in front is decided per pixel by a depth buffer, not
by the order entities are listed in, and it keeps far things apart:
natively two surfaces 1 cm apart are told apart at 500 m. In a browser on
**WebGL2** (no WebGPU) depth is less precise — 1 cm apart is still told
apart at 50 m with the default 0.1 m near plane, but a scene whose far
surfaces flicker against each other should raise `near` (`docs/WEB.md`).

### `MeshInstance3D`

```ron
"MeshInstance3D": (
    mesh: "builtin:sphere",
    color: (0.1, 0.8, 0.2, 1.0),
    material: Some(Path("materials/gold.material.ron")),
),
```

- **`mesh`**: one of the built-in meshes — `builtin:cube`, `builtin:sphere`,
  `builtin:plane`, `builtin:capsule`, `builtin:cylinder` — or a mesh file
  under the asset root. A misspelt `builtin:` name stops the scene loading
  with the list of the ones there are; a file that will not load draws a
  magenta cube and says so once in the Console. (Mesh files, glTF, are
  read from a later stage; until then a file always draws magenta.)
- **`color`**: linear RGBA, white by default: it multiplies the
  material's base colour.
- **`material`**: a surface material (below); none — the default — is a
  white dielectric of roughness 0.5. A material that will not load draws
  as that default and says why once in the Console.

The built-in meshes are a metre across, so a scale is a size in metres:
the cube, sphere and cylinder fit a 1 m box about their centre, the plane
is a 1 m square facing up, and the capsule is 2 m tall and 1 m wide — a
person. Each has the normals, UVs and tangents a material needs.

A mesh with no `Transform3D` sits at its parent's origin, or the world's.
Every copy of one mesh in view is drawn in one go, and a mesh outside the
camera's view costs no drawing at all, so thousands are fine.

## Materials and light

A mesh's surface is a `.material.ron` of `domain: Surface` — the same file
format as a sprite material, which is `domain: Sprite` and the
default, so a sprite material written before 3D loads as it was. Its
`standard` block is glTF 2.0's metallic-roughness material, field for
field, so a model's materials (from a later stage) map onto it one to one:

```ron
// assets/materials/gold.material.ron
(
    version: 1,
    domain: Surface,
    standard: (
        base_color: (1.0, 0.77, 0.34, 1.0),
        metallic: 1.0,
        roughness: 0.25,
    ),
)
```

Every field can be left out:

| Field | Default | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `base_color`, `base_color_texture` | white, none | the colour, linear, times the image (sRGB) and the mesh's `color` |
| `metallic`, `roughness`, `metallic_roughness_texture` | 0, 0.5, none | 0 a dielectric, 1 a metal; 0 a mirror, 1 chalk; the image's green channel scales roughness, its blue metallic |
| `normal_texture`, `normal_scale` | none, 1 | a tangent-space normal map (green up, glTF's convention) and how far it bends |
| `occlusion_texture`, `occlusion_strength` | none, 1 | its red channel darkens the ambient |
| `emissive`, `emissive_texture`, `emissive_strength` | black, none, 1 | light the surface gives off, lit or not |
| `alpha` | `Opaque` | `Mask(0.5)` cuts out under the cutoff; `Blend` lays the surface over what is behind |
| `double_sided` | `false` | both faces drawn, the back lit as the front turned round |
| `unlit` | `false` | the base colour drawn as it is, no light |
| `uv_transform` | none | `(offset: (u, v), scale: (u, v), rotation: radians)`, for every texture |

A texture is a path from the asset root. **Colour textures** (base colour,
emissive) are read as sRGB, like every picture; **data textures**
(metallic-roughness, normal, occlusion) are read as their bytes — a normal
map is numbers, not a colour. Lighting is Filament's: GGX highlights,
height-correlated Smith, Schlick Fresnel, Lambert diffuse, the model glTF
viewers and Blender's EEVEE share, so a material looks as its author's
tool showed it. A blended surface is drawn after every opaque one,
farthest first; a masked one has smooth edges under MSAA.

`scenes/materials3d.ron` shows them: four spheres from a rough dielectric
to a smooth metal, an unlit one, a floor bent by a normal map, a grate cut
out by its alpha, an emissive lamp and a glass pane. Its materials are in
`assets/materials/`.

**Light.** One `DirectionalLight3D` lights the world, along its entity's
−Z (with several, the one on the lowest entity index; a later stage
lights them all):

```ron
"DirectionalLight3D": (color: (1.0, 0.96, 0.9), energy: 2.0),
```

`energy` 1 (the default) is a sun; it is stored so that switching the
project to physical light units later shows the same light in lux,
unchanged. An `Environment3D` gives the ambient — the light of a uniform
sky, what lights a face turned from the sun:

```ron
"Environment3D": (ambient: (0.12, 0.14, 0.18)),
```

On a camera's entity it is that camera's; anywhere else it is the
world's, for every camera without one. With none, the ambient is a dim
0.1 grey, so a scene with no light is not black; an `Environment3D` of
`(0.0, 0.0, 0.0)` makes the dark. A metal reflects only its surroundings,
so with nothing but the ambient around it, it is dark — a sky that
lights is a later stage.

**In the editor**, a mesh's inspector names its material — typed, or
dropped from the asset browser, where a surface material's tile is the
material on a sphere — and **New surface material** writes one into
`materials/`. Under it, **surface material** edits the file itself: a
field per input, colour pickers (linear, shown in sRGB), texture slots you
drop images on, and a live preview on a lit sphere. The file is shared by
every mesh naming it, as a material is in Unity and Godot: an edit is
written when you let go and every mesh changes at once; it is not part of
the scene's undo.

## One frame, both worlds

A scene can hold 2D and 3D cameras together, and every active one draws.
**Priority is the order**: lowest first, each over the ones before it
where they overlap, and **at equal priority a 3D camera draws before a 2D
one**. So with every camera at the default priority 0 the 3D world is the
backdrop and the 2D world draws over it — and the HUD (chapter 5) draws
once, over everything:

```text
priority 0   Camera3D   the 3D world, through its tone curve
priority 0   Camera2D   the 2D world over it, see-through where it draws nothing
             the HUD    over both
```

`scenes/mixed3d.ron` is that frame: a floor, a cube and a sphere; a 2D
banner and a half-transparent 2D badge over them; a HUD panel over all.

**Overlay or opaque.** A camera drawn over part of a picture of the other
dimension is an **overlay**: its picture starts transparent, so what was
drawn before shows wherever it draws nothing — a 2D world over a 3D one,
or a 3D model over a 2D game. A `Camera3D` with `clear: Color(...)` starts
from its colour instead and hides what is under it. A 2D camera's light
(`ambient`, chapter 4) lights its own sprites, not the 3D world under
them; its grade (`post`) grades its own layer, soft edges included.

**Several cameras of one kind.** As in 2D (chapter 2), a camera wholly
under a later full-screen camera *of the same kind*, with no camera of the
other kind between them, is not drawn — several framings of one world, the
highest wins. A 2D camera never hides a 3D one, or the reverse, and a 3D
camera at priority 0, a 2D one at 1 and a 3D one at 2 all draw: three
layers, not framings.

Some frames, by priority:

- **A 3D character over a 2D menu**: the menu's `Camera2D` at priority 0,
  the `Camera3D` at 1 with a `viewport` in a corner. With `clear:
  Background` the model floats over the menu; with a colour it is a framed
  picture in a picture.
- **Split screen**: a `Camera3D` with `viewport` on the left half, a
  `Camera2D` on the right; neither overlaps the other, so both are
  opaque.
- **A 3D minimap**: a `Camera3D` with a `CameraTarget` naming a
  `.rendertexture.ron`, shown by a sprite or a HUD image naming the same
  file (chapter 2's render textures). A camera drawing into a texture
  draws before every camera whose world may show that texture. A
  `clear: Color` with an alpha below 1 leaves the texture see-through
  there, and the sprite showing it blends it once.

**Tone curves and HDR.** A 3D camera draws into a high-range target —
light can add up past 1 — and its `tonemap` brings that into the screen's
range after `post.exposure` and bloom, before the rest of the grade. `AgX`,
the default, rolls bright colours off towards white as film does; `Aces`
is more contrasty; `Reinhard` gentle; `None` (a save writes it `r#None`,
`None` being a word of RON's own; either reads) clips at 1 and leaves a
colour exactly as written — what `scenes/shapes3d.ron` uses so its flat
colours read back as given. 2D cameras have no curve.

**Smooth edges.** 3D edges are smoothed by 4× multisampling, set for the
project in `assets/default.render.ron` (Project Settings ▸ Render):

```ron
(version: 1, msaa: 4)
```

`msaa: 1` turns it off. A browser on WebGL2 that cannot draw the
high-range target or multisample it falls back — 8 bits, or one sample —
and says so once in the console (`docs/WEB.md`).

## Not yet

A 3D view, point and spot lights and several suns, shadows, a sky that
lights, custom surface shaders, glTF models and 3D physics are the coming
stages of 0.5. Textures have no mipmaps yet, so a finely detailed texture
far away shimmers. A 2D camera has no clear colour of its own yet, so a 2D
camera over a 3D one is always an overlay. A `Transform3D` travels as plain numbers (40
bytes) until its compact wire form, and the Network window's map shows 2D
entities only.
