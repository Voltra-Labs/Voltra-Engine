# 11. 3D

0.5 adds a 3D world beside the 2D one. It is being built stage by stage,
and this chapter grows with it; what is here now is the part every later
3D feature stands on: where things are, and how they hang off each other.

There is no 3D view in the editor yet, and nothing in 3D draws. A scene
can hold 3D entities, save them, load them, parent them and spawn them
from prefabs; the editor's scene view stays 2D and says in its toolbar how
many 3D entities it is not showing.

## Two worlds in one scene

2D and 3D are two parallel subsystems in one engine, the way Godot keeps
`Node2D` and `Node3D`: a 2D entity carries a `Transform2D`, a 3D one a
`Transform3D`, and everything else — names, the hierarchy, prefabs,
scenes, audio, input, the UI — is shared. Nothing locks a project to one:
a scene holds whichever entities it holds.

2D is never 3D flattened. A `Transform2D` has no z, the 2D renderer has no
depth buffer, and draw order in 2D is still `sort_order`.

## Coordinates

- **Y is up, the world is right-handed, forward is −Z, and a unit is a
  metre.** That is glTF's convention (and Godot's and Bevy's), so a model
  exported from Blender as glTF arrives the right way up and the right
  size with no conversion.
- Up means the same in both worlds: 2D is Y up too.
- A camera and a light, when they come, look down their entity's −Z.

## `Transform3D`

```ron
"Transform3D": (
    translation: (0.0, 1.5, -4.0),
    rotation: (0.0, 0.70710677, 0.0, 0.70710677),
    scale: (1.0, 1.0, 1.0),
),
```

- **`translation`**: where the entity is, in metres, relative to its parent.
- **`rotation`**: a quaternion `(x, y, z, w)` — exact, no gimbal lock. A
  file is text you can edit: a quaternion typed by hand that is not quite
  unit length is normalised as it loads, and one that is not a rotation at
  all (all zeros) loads as no rotation. The Console says so once.
- **`scale`**: per axis; `(1, 1, 1)` leaves the size.

Any field left out takes its default, so `"Transform3D": (translation: (0.0,
1.0, 0.0))` is a whole transform.

In the **inspector**, the rotation is three angles in degrees — about X, Y
and Z, applied Y first, then X, then Z (yaw, pitch, roll; Godot's order).
What you type stays what you see: a yaw of 180 stays 180, though the
quaternion underneath could be read back as a pitch and a roll of 180 each.

In code:

```rust
use voltra_scene::Transform3D;
use voltra_render::glam::Vec3;

let lamp = Transform3D::from_translation(Vec3::new(0.0, 2.0, 0.0));
let turned = Transform3D::from_euler_degrees(Vec3::new(0.0, 90.0, 0.0));
let aimed = lamp.looking_at(Vec3::ZERO, Vec3::Y);
assert!(aimed.forward().y < 0.0); // looking down at the origin
```

`forward()`, `right()` and `up()` are the entity's −Z, +X and +Y in its
parent's space; `matrix()` is the `Affine3A` it composes as (scale, then
rotate, then translate).

## Making a 3D entity

**Hierarchy ▸ Create ▸ 3D ▸ Empty** makes one at the origin, named
`Empty3D`. Give it components in the inspector, or through the editor's
bridge and the MCP server, which set `Transform3D` like any other component.

## Parenting

A `Transform3D` is local to its parent, as a `Transform2D` is: move the
parent and the children come with it, turned by its rotation and grown by
its scale. `hierarchy::world_matrix_3d` gives one entity's composed matrix;
`WorldTransforms3D::from_world` composes the whole world in one pass.

The rules:

- **A chain keeps to one dimension.** A 3D entity cannot be the child of a
  2D one, or the parent of one; the hierarchy panel shows such a drop in
  red and refuses it. There is nothing to compose: a 2D offset means
  nothing in 3D.
- **An entity with no transform passes the rule through.** A bare entity
  can group either kind — but once it holds 3D children it cannot go under
  a 2D parent, and the reverse.
- **An entity is 2D or 3D, not both.** The inspector and the bridge will
  not add a `Transform3D` to an entity that has a `Transform2D`.
- A file that breaks a rule anyway (a hand edit, a merge) opens with a
  warning in the Console and is left as it is; each world composes the
  entity by its own transform, as if the other one's link were not there.
- Physics and parenting stay exclusive in 2D, and there is no 3D physics
  yet.

## Prefabs

A 3D prefab works as a 2D one does. Spawn it where it should stand by
passing a `Transform3D`:

```rust
let crate_prefab = tick.prefab(&AssetPath::new("prefabs/crate.prefab.ron")?);
let at = Transform3D::from_translation(Vec3::new(4.0, 0.0, -2.0));
tick.spawn(crate_prefab, at)?;
```

or `Placement::AsAuthored` to leave its roots where the file put them. A
2D placement does not move a 3D root (it stays where its file put it, with
a warning), so dropping a 3D prefab on the 2D scene view spawns it as
authored.

As in 2D, an instance root's transform is its placement and never an
override: move it and nothing is recorded against the prefab, **Apply**
keeps the file's own root transform, and **Create prefab** from a 3D
selection stores the root at the origin with its rotation and scale kept.

## Animation and networking

A clip keys a 3D transform with `Translation3D`, `Rotation3D` and
`Scale3D`. A rotation key is a quaternion, `Quat((x, y, z, w))` in the
file; between two keys it turns the short way round, so key a spin every
quarter turn or so. Crossfades, blend trees and layers mix rotations by
`slerp`, and an additive layer adds its turn on top of what is under it:

```ron
(looping: true, length: 2.0, tracks: [
    (property: Rotation3D, keys: [
        (time: 0.0, value: Quat((0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0))),
        (time: 1.0, value: Quat((0.0, 0.7071068, 0.0, 0.7071068))),
        (time: 2.0, value: Quat((0.0, 1.0, 0.0, 0.0))),
    ]),
])
```

The Timeline lists the three properties and edits a rotation key as Euler
degrees; authoring 3D clips properly comes with the 3D view.

Over the network a `Transform3D` is replicated as a `Transform2D` is:
remote copies are drawn between snapshots, your own predicted player is
corrected to the server, and interest management culls by distance in
space (chapter 8).

## Not yet

A 3D view, cameras, meshes, lights, materials, glTF models and 3D physics
are the coming stages of 0.5. A `Transform3D` travels as plain numbers (40
bytes) until its compact wire form, and the Network window's map shows 2D
entities only.
